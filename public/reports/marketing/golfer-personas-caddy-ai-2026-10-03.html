Part 1

The Personas

Seven audiences on three different axes — how they shop, where they play, and why they buy. The four core personas are the shopping-style segments already in the product-marketing context; the three new segments and two overlays extend the map. All are proxy-sourced from golf-industry data and public community voice, and are provisional until validated against the first Founding Members.

Core — the four shopping-style personas

The Everyday Golfer

Core

Wants trustworthy picks and simpler shopping — loves golf but won't research for hours.

The Gear & Performance Golfer

Core

Comparisons, specs, new vs. used, multiple buying options — buys right, once.

The Golf Lifestyle Golfer

Core

Apparel, travel, courses, events, brands and content — golf as a way of life.

The New / Returning Golfer

Core

Where to start, what's enough, what things cost — golf feels complicated and expensive.

New — three segments the core four miss

The Social Golfer

NewConfidence: High

Off-course and entertainment-first — Topgolf, simulators and the range, where golf is a social outing, not a scorecard.

Profile
Younger, the most gender-balanced segment (43% of off-course-only players are women); one of ~19M who played golf only off-course in 2025. May rarely play 18.
Job to be done
Functional: look good and have fun at the bay without a "serious golfer" setup. Emotional: belong, not be judged. Social: be the friend with the cool gear.
Triggers
A Topgolf birthday or bachelor party · buying a home simulator or Garmin R10 · a corporate outing · "everyone's into golf now."
Top pains
Golf retail feels built for low-handicap men; doesn't know what to buy first; nothing speaks to the sim/range crowd.
Desired outcome
A confident first purchase with no fitting; gear that looks good and works off-course; a low-pressure on-ramp into the game.
Objections
"Is this for serious golfers only?" · "Will I get upsold clubs I don't need?"
Alternatives
Amazon · Topgolf pro shop · apparel brands · big-box · borrowing.
Vocabulary
"I'm not a real golfer but…" · "just for Topgolf" · "getting into golf" · "home setup."
Reach
Instagram / TikTok · venues · lifestyle creators; content like "what to wear to Topgolf," "best starter setup," "home sim guide."
CHG fit
Apparel · Tech · Simulators · Practice; the DISCOVER / lifestyle territory. The largest growth segment the core four ignore.

The Equipment Enthusiast

NewConfidence: Medium

The gear-obsessed golfer — collectors chasing limited and tour-only pieces, and tinkerers always buying the next club that'll fix their game. Highest lifetime value, loudest community voice.

Profile
Mid-to-low handicap, deeply brand-aware, lives in forums and on YouTube. Two motivations to serve together: collectors (Scotty Cameron, Bettinardi, limited/tour-only) and tinkerers (chasing the next fix).
Job to be done
Functional: find, compare and acquire specific gear — new, used and rare — faster than anyone. Emotional: the hunt and the new-club high. Social: "what's in the bag" status.
Triggers
A product drop · a tour win on a club · a rare used listing · a bad round · sell-to-upgrade cycles.
Top pains
Deals, drops and rare used inventory scattered across a dozen sites and forums; can't compare new vs. used for the same spec in one place; misses limited releases.
Desired outcome
One place to track, compare and pounce (new + used, across sellers); price history; drop and restock alerts; trusted used-condition grading.
Objections
"Will your used specs and condition be accurate?" · "Do you have the rare stuff, or just mainstream retail?"
Alternatives
GolfWRX BST · 2nd Swing / GlobalGolf · eBay · Facebook Marketplace.
Vocabulary
"WITB" · "BST" · "tour issue" · "gamer" · "grail" · "slot in the bag."
Reach
GolfWRX · r/golf · YouTube reviewers · Discord. Ideal Clubhouse Feed seed users.
CHG fit
The core of the cross-brand / new-plus-used comparison wedge, and prime Feed fuel. Distinct from the Gear & Performance Golfer, who buys right once; the Enthusiast buys constantly.

The Improver

NewConfidence: Medium

The data-and-improvement golfer — measures the game, finds the weakness, buys or books the fix, and checks it worked.

Profile
Committed golfer chasing lower scores; uses GHIN, Arccos / Shot Scope, 18Birdies and a launch monitor. Buys gear in service of measured improvement, plus lessons and training aids.
Job to be done
Functional: measure → find the weakness → buy/learn the fix → verify. Emotional: confidence that effort and money are translating into lower scores. Social: the handicap number as a badge.
Triggers
A plateau or bad stretch · a launch-monitor session exposing a gap · a lesson recommending a change · a new-season goal.
Top pains
Data, lessons, gear and coaching live in separate apps and sites — the insight never connects to the purchase or the drill.
Desired outcome
The connected loop: see the weakness → get the fix (gear, drill or coach) → buy or book it → confirm it worked.
Alternatives
Arccos · GHIN / TheGrint · 18Birdies · Skillest / GOLFTEC · MyGolfSpy test data.
Vocabulary
"strokes gained" · "gapping" · "dialing it in" · "break 80" · "dispersion" · "my miss."
Reach
MyGolfSpy · the Arccos / 18Birdies ecosystems · instruction YouTube; Caddy AI Q&A.
CHG fit
The IMPROVE layer — virtual tutoring, the coach directory, launch monitors (Tech). The persona the connected-loop story was built for.

Honesty flag. On-course tracking (the Course tab) is out of Phase 1. Clubhouse can serve this persona's improve → decide → buy loop, but not on-course shot tracking at launch — so don't promise Caddy AI can track their round.

Overlays — two lenses, not standalone personas

The Modern Woman Golfer

Overlay · lens

Nearly 8M women golfers and a fifth straight year of growth; women are 35% of beginners and 35% of juniors. Distinct pains — gear built and marketed for men, poor fit and selection. Not a seventh box: a lens every persona, filter and line of copy must pass.

The Golf Gifter

Overlay · occasion

Someone buying golf for a golfer — partner, parent, corporate — often low on category knowledge and seasonal (holidays, Father's Day). Every other persona assumes the shopper is the player; this is the exception. Needs "help me pick," gift cards and reassuring returns.

Part 2

The marketing-psychology layer

The highest-leverage mental model for each new segment and overlay — the lever that actually changes behaviour, not a list of everything that could apply.

The Social Golfer

New to the category and easily overwhelmed. Curate, don't catalogue: one recommended starter kit, plain language, and a free-shipping / free-returns hook to lower the risk of a first buy.

Paradox of Choice · Default Effect · Zero-Price Effect · Curse of Knowledge

The Equipment Enthusiast

Driven by the hunt and the next release. Drop calendars and restock / price-drop alerts open loops they can't leave unclosed; a "build your bag" configurator turns effort into attachment.

Scarcity · Zeigarnik (open loops) · Endowment / IKEA Effect · Loss Aversion

The Improver

Motivated by progress. Frame the gap as nearly closed, start with a trivially small first step (a free question to Caddy), and lend authority through named coaches.

Goal-Gradient · Commitment & Consistency · Authority

The Modern Woman Golfer (lens)

Speak as one of them, not down to them. Relatable voice and imagery, and an honest admission that golf retail wasn't built for everyone — which Clubhouse is fixing.

Unity · Liking / Similarity · Pratfall Effect

The Golf Gifter (occasion)

Anxious about picking wrong for someone else. Remove the regret: three price-anchored bundles, a guided "help me pick," gift cards and easy returns.

Regret Aversion · Anchoring · Paradox of Choice

The cross-cutting insight. Every golfer persona shares one enemy — overwhelm and fragmentation, Clubhouse's own stated problem. So Hick's Law, Paradox of Choice and Activation Energy are the master models for all of them: fewer, smarter options beat more. That is exactly the job Caddy AI should do.

Part 3

How to use this for Caddy AI

Personas earn their keep when something acts on them in real time. Caddy AI — the Clubhouse AI helper — is that actuator. It infers a golfer's need-state from the first messages (it never shows a label) and adapts how it answers.

Persona → Caddy behaviour routing. Caddy detects a need-state and adjusts tone, depth, how many options it shows, and the default next step.

Five ways Caddy uses the personas

  1. Intent routing, not labels

    Caddy classifies the golfer's need-state from the first messages and adapts tone, depth, option count and default CTA per the table above — without ever showing the golfer a persona label.

  2. The fix for the universal pain

    Overwhelm is the shared enemy. Caddy applies Hick's Law and Activation Energy live, turning ten tabs and two hundred SKUs into one good next step — the single most on-strategy thing it can do.

  3. A live first-party research engine

    Every conversation is unprompted, first-party signal: which need-states actually appear, in what words, how often. Fed back into these personas, it converts the whole set from proxy-based to evidence-based within weeks of launch.

  4. Ethical psychology, in conversation

    Goal-Gradient for Improvers ("you're close"), Reciprocity for everyone (answer the question first, options second), Authority via named coaches, and honest Social Proof ("most golfers in your spot choose…").

  5. Launch guardrails

    Caddy is off-course only at launch — no club-from-shot, no GPS, no "knows your game." It answers the question before showing anything to buy, and discloses any commercial relationship near a recommendation. Its exact answer scope is an open decision for launch (product-marketing Open Question 11).

Why it matters commercially. A persona-aware Caddy lowers activation energy and decision friction at the exact moment of intent — moving the North Star (a completed order on Clubhouse) more than any extra persona on a slide ever will.
