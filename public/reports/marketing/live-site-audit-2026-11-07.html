Live Site Audit — Red-Teamed

A live walk-through of www.chgolfco.com three weeks after launch, then every finding re-tested before publication. The first pass produced sixteen findings; seven did not survive verification and are retracted in full at the end. What remains is five P0 issues, four P1 and five P2 — and the cheapest of them to fix is a title tag.

Part 1

The verdict

The platform works. Discovery, faceting and the community all function when you use them. What does not work is everything a machine sees — and most of that is fixable without touching the architecture.

One sentence: the site renders nothing on the server, serves the same <title> on every page, and lists only eight legal URLs in its sitemap — so a catalogue of 3,390 club models and ~350 brands is effectively invisible to search. Separately, one struck-through price sits above the manufacturer’s own MSRP, which is a legal exposure rather than a UX one.

Cheapest fix, largest upside. Unique per-page titles, and a sitemap that lists the category pages. Neither requires the re-platform that the performance findings imply. Both could ship this week.
Catalogue: 3,390 club models · ~350 brands · URLs in sitemap: 8 · crawlable product links: 0
Part 2

P0 — fix before spending on traffic

Every row was directly observed. The check is named so each one can be re-run.

P0 findings, with the check that produced them
#FindingStatusHow it was verified
1 No server-rendered content on any route Confirmed curl /shop/clubs returns HTML with no product name, no price, and not the string 3,390 — only <title>Clubhouse Golf</title>. This is the root cause of findings 2–4.
2 Identical <title> on every page Confirmed Seven routes checked at raw-HTML level (/, /shop, /shop/clubs, /shop/balls, /feed, /course, /brands) — all serve Clubhouse Golf as both <title> and og:title. Canonicals, by contrast, are correct and per-page.
3 Sitemap contains 8 URLs, all legal boilerplate Confirmed <loc> count is exactly 8: home, privacy, terms, shipping, returns, warranty, accessibility, delete-account. Absent: /shop, every category, /brands, /feed, /course, every product. /sitemap-products.xml and /sitemap_index.xml return the SPA shell, not XML, so this is the only sitemap.
4 Product cards are not links Confirmed 39 elements on /shop/clubs carry role="link" with no href. Enumerated, they are the primary nav, the breadcrumb and ~24 product cards. Only 12 real href anchors exist, all category and brand pills. No product is reachable by a crawlable link, and a shopper cannot open a club in a new tab.
5 Reference price above manufacturer MSRP Confirmed Site shows Callaway Elyte Driver $299.98, was $699.99. Callaway’s own launch pricing is $599.99 (Standard / X / Max Fast) and $649.99 for Triple Diamond. The “was” price is ~$100 above MSRP.
Finding 5 needs legal review, not design review. A struck-through reference price that was never a bona fide selling price is a recognised FTC deceptive-pricing pattern. The fix is to audit every strikethrough in the feed, not just this one. For contrast, the Titleist GT3 at $449.99 from $649.99 is consistent with that model’s MSRP — so this is a data problem in parts of the feed, not everywhere.

P1 — performance and credibility

Read the shape, not just the scores. FCP is excellent on both — the shell paints almost immediately, then the page stalls for seconds before anything useful appears. That is the signature of a client-rendered app, and it is the same root cause as finding 1. Desktop LCP at 7.6s is still three times the “good” threshold, and CLS exceeds 0.1 on both.

7. Product images are hotlinked from third parties

Of 26 images on /shop/clubs: 12 from Callaway’s Salesforce CDN, 6 from fairwaygolfusa.com, 4 from dks.scene7.com (DICK’S), 2 from performancegolf.com, and only 2 self-hosted. You cannot resize, convert or cache-control these, and the grid breaks if any supplier changes a path. Whether this also breaches those retailers’ terms was not checked.

8. Cards omit the retailer, so identical names show unexplained price gaps

Two cards both read “CALLAWAY GOLF Elyte Driver”, at $299.98 and $399.99. A RETAILER facet does exist, so multiple listings per model is deliberate marketplace behaviour — but the card never names the seller, leaving the shopper no reason for the difference. Fix the card, not the data.

9. Cart shows “1” on an anonymous load

Observed on /, /shop and /shop/clubs, persisting across routes with no item ever added. Either seeded data or a state bug — either way it will distort every funnel metric and every checkout test until it is cleared.

Part 4

P2 — worth doing

10 · Dead alias

/talk is not a route. The community lives at /feed and loads correctly on direct navigation — but the nav label says TALK, so chgolfco.com/talk, the URL a person would guess, type or print on a flyer, renders “Unmatched Route — Page could not be found.” Add a redirect. /feed is also missing from the sitemap.

11 · Document outline

/shop/clubs has exactly one heading on the entire page, and it reads “Filter by”. No other h1, h2, h3 or role="heading" exists anywhere. The category name is not a heading.

12 · Accessibility — 91/100

Lighthouse flags insufficient colour contrast and missing alt attributes. Separately confirmed on /shop/clubs: 2 of 26 images lack alt.

13 · Back/forward cache blocked

Returning to a product grid re-pays the full load cost, which on mobile is the 24s measured above.

14 · Lessons are buried — opportunity, not defect

/course contains Play / Lessons / Bookings with mobile-first GPS and live scoring. It works. But nobody searching for golf lessons will guess that “COURSE” is where lessons live. Surface it in the primary nav, or cross-link from the community.

Part 5

Red team — what the first pass got wrong

Seven of the sixteen findings in v1 did not survive re-testing. They are listed in full rather than quietly deleted, because the pattern matters more than the individual errors.

Retractions from v1
#v1 claimVerdictWhat is actually true
R1 “/talk is a soft 404, so the community is unindexable and unshareable” Wrong The community is at /feed and loads fine on direct navigation. v1 asserted “TALK only works via in-app clicks” without ever clicking TALK. Downgraded from P0 to P2 finding 10.
R2 “LCP 24.1s on cold desktop, good connection” Mislabelled That run was throttled mobile emulation — Lighthouse CLI’s default, and --preset=desktop was never passed. Desktop LCP is 7.6s. Both numbers are bad, so the conclusion stood; the label did not.
R3 “No shaft flex or loft facet” Wrong LOFT (5.5°–9.5°, “show 41 more”), SHAFT (Graphite / Steel), FLEX (Lite → Extra Stiff) and CONDITION all exist. v1 read a facet list truncated at PRICE and assumed it ended there.
R4 “Brand filter polluted with merchant names” Wrong A dedicated RETAILER facet exists, and Golf Galaxy, Fairway Golf Inc and Callaway Pre Owned sit there correctly. The BRAND facet is clean. Residual nit only: “Puma Golf And Cobra Golf” reads as a feed name rather than a retailer, and the /shop “350 brands” list does mix in retailers.
R5 “Merchant feeds are not deduped by SKU” Overstated Multi-retailer listings per model are deliberate, with a Compare affordance. Replaced by the narrower and accurate finding 8.
R6 “/partner and /become-a-partner both return 200; one is a soft 404” Meaningless /zzz-does-not-exist-9f3 also returns 200. Every path does — normal SPA behaviour. v1 had no control test, so status codes carried no information at all.
R7 “The header notification bell likely does nothing” Overstated The console warning concerns push-token change listeners on web only, not the notification UI. Untested either way.
The pattern. Every error came from inferring a cause from partial output instead of checking it: a truncated facet list, an unverified tool configuration, a route never clicked, a status code with no control. One false positive was caught during the original pass only because a network log contradicted it; six more needed a second pass. Any automated or agent-driven version of this audit will produce the same class of error with nobody auditing its reasoning — which is the argument for keeping a verification step rather than scaling the first pass.
Part 6

Method & coverage

What was done

Live browser walk-through of the production site with DOM and network inspection; Lighthouse on mobile and desktop presets; curl against raw server HTML to separate server-rendered from client-rendered content; a control request to a deliberately invalid path; and one external price check against the manufacturer’s published MSRP.

What was not covered

Caddy AI answer quality; signup and onboarding completion (no mailbox was available to confirm email verification); checkout beyond add-to-cart; a full mobile-viewport walk-through; the Buying Guides → purchase path; and the BECOME A PARTNER funnel. None of these were assessed, and no conclusion about them should be drawn from this report.

Analytics note. These sessions were tracked by the live GA4 property (G-X9SXYER9WP) — page_view and scroll events fired against /shop/clubs. Filter 7 November 2026 before reading November funnel data.
