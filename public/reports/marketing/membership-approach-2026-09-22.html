Membership Approach — Phase 1

Three membership models were on the table. This brief recommends one for 15 October — Founding Member, partner-funded, free — built as a badge and an account flag only, and explains why the calendar, not the strategy, decides it.

Section 1

The recommendation

Decision needed before 29 September

Ship Founding Member as a badge and an account flag. Nothing else. Everything that touches money — cashback, wallet credit, member pricing, a paid tier — is deferred to Phase 2 and gated behind the criteria in §6.

Of the three membership models on the table, only one can ship on 15 October without putting the date at risk: Founding Member, partner-funded, free. And it only stays safe if it ships in its thinnest possible form.

In Phase 1 Deferred
Account flag Founding Member status on the member account, set by staff Tiered entitlements
Badge A visual badge on the member's Feed posts and profile Member-only surfaces beyond the badge
Meaning Early access and influence: a direct line into what gets built Member pricing, early-drop windows
Money None Cashback, wallet credit, paid tiers, coaching bundles
Copy "One of the first 500. You help decide what we build." Anything implying a discount or a price

What this is: a recognition and access program for the first 500 golfers, funded by nothing, promising nothing we do not already control.

What this is not: a loyalty program, a discount club or a subscription. Those are real options — they are Phase 2 options, for the reasons below.

Section 2

Why this is the smart call for this timeline

Seven days

From today, 22 September, there are seven days to app feature freeze. The build proposal is explicit: nine weeks “is achievable on the scope written here. It is not achievable if the scope grows mid-build. Change requests after design sign-off move the launch date.”

The calendar decides this, not the strategy

Date Gate Days from today
22 Sep 2026 Today —
29 Sep App feature freeze 7
1 Oct Builds submitted to Apple and Google 9
7–14 Oct Client UAT, fixes, catalog freeze 15–22
15 Oct Launch — website live, app subject to store review 23

Apple's review is not ours to control, and a rejection resets the clock. Anything that lands in the app after 29 September does not ship on time.

Every richer model lands in the riskiest code

A badge and a flag touch the member account. Cashback, wallet credit and member pricing touch the wallet, refunds, the platform fee and per-partner settlement — the parts of Phase 1 that handle real money, and the parts where a defect is not a cosmetic bug but a financial one. Adding them in the final week would mean:

  • changing money-handling code after the point where there is time to test it properly;
  • a refund path that now has to claw back credit already issued;
  • a settlement report that has to account for a discount nobody has agreed with a partner yet.

It is the only model that survives having no signed partners

No fulfillment partner is signed yet. A partner-funded perk is a promise written against someone else's signature. The badge is the one piece of the program that is true on 15 October regardless of how the partner conversations land.

It keeps the launch promise small and true

Pre-launch, the credible claim is access, not value. "You are one of the first 500 and you help decide what gets built" is verifiable on day one. "Members save money" is not, and the moment it is printed it has to be honoured.

Section 3

Why free, and why partner-funded later

Free is the market default, not a concession. 18Birdies has a free tier at 10M self-reported users, GHIN is free to handicap golfers, r/golf and GolfWRX are free. A paid tier over a catalog that is still thin — and a checkout that still splits on referral items — reads as a toll booth on an unfinished road.

There is no traffic to convert yet. Subscription economics need an audience; Clubhouse has a waitlist. Charging the first 500 golfers, who are being asked to give feedback, inverts the relationship.

The eventual paywall is already visible. Caddy AI costs money per message, and that cost scales with usage rather than revenue. When a paid tier is right, unlimited Caddy AI is the natural thing behind it — not a discount on gear, which is the partner's margin to give.

Partner-funded perks are the right Phase 2 shape — early access to drops, member offers, wallet credit on completed orders — because they are paid for by the supply side rather than by the golfer. They just cannot be promised before the partners exist.

Section 4

What we are deliberately not doing, and why

Five traps

Each of these looked attractive and has a concrete reason to wait. None is a marketing judgement call; four are commercial or legal.

1. Cashback on orders

Funded out of a platform fee that is not yet set. On referral items the commission is as low as 3% (Amazon Associates) and arrives 60–80 days later, so cashback there can cost more than the order earns. As merchant of record, every refund then needs a clawback path.

2. Member-only pricing

Brands enforce minimum advertised price. A member discount on a brand's product can breach MAP — and MAP enforcement is already a stated partner anxiety. The perk that wins golfers could be the thing that loses the brand.

3. A paid digital tier in the app

Physical goods sold through Stripe avoid Apple's commission, which is why the build is compliant today. A digital subscription (unlimited Caddy AI, training content) generally does not — Apple expects in-app purchase, at up to 30%. That changes the economics of any paid tier and needs checking before one is designed, not after.

4. Wallet credit as a member perk

Stored value can attract gift-card, unclaimed-property and revenue-recognition rules. A wallet that holds refunds is one thing; a wallet that issues promotional credit is another. Legal review first.

5. "Free lifetime membership"

The proposal promises the first 500 a free lifetime membership; the live site says "Join Waitlist"; the context document says free access and influence. Committing to lifetime now either gives away the future paid tier to the most valuable 500 golfers, or forces a public promise to be walked back. Say "founding access", not "lifetime", until this is decided (Open Question 8).

Section 5

What Founding Member means in copy

One promise, said the same way everywhere:

Founding Members are the first 500 golfers inside Clubhouse. You get in early, and you help decide what gets built.

Say: first 500 · founding access · early access to what we build next · a direct line to the team · your feedback shapes the product · member badge in the Feed.

Don't say: lifetime · free forever · member pricing · members save · exclusive deals · discount · VIP.

Two rules that follow from the launch scope:

  • No sample content may show member pricing. The TestFlight Feed currently includes a demo post about "Founding Member pricing on the new driver lineup". That post cannot ship.
  • The badge is a label, not a claim. It says who someone is. It must not imply a benefit that has not been agreed with a partner.

Align the site too: the live site's "Join Waitlist" and the internal "Become a Founding Member" should say the same thing by launch.

Section 6

When membership comes back — the gates

Membership returns as a real product when the business can support it. Three gates, all measurable:

Gate Threshold Why it matters
Supply 3+ fulfillment partners live Perks and cashback need margin and a catalog worth joining for
Community Sustained weekly posting in the Feed, most posts answered A badge signals nothing in an empty room
Terms Platform fee and payout schedule set You cannot fund a perk out of an undefined margin

Cheap tests to run before any of it is built — none of these touch the app:

  1. Ask the waitlist what they would actually pay for. One question, one email.
  2. Put a "Clubhouse Plus — register interest" link on the site and count clicks.
  3. Ask the first five partner conversations what member benefit they would fund, and what breaches their MAP policy.

Phase 2 sequence, once the gates are met: entitlements on the member account → partner-funded perks (early access, member offers) → wallet credit on completed orders → a paid tier whose paywall is Caddy AI usage, priced against 18Birdies Premium ($99.99/yr) and GolfPass ($99–119/yr — the two figures in our sources disagree; re-verify before quoting).

Section 7

What this depends on, and what would change the answer

Open decisions this brief assumes (it does not resolve them)

# Open decision Effect if it moves
1 Platform fee and payout schedule Sets whether any funded perk is affordable at all
4 Disclosure standard Any member benefit tied to a recommendation needs it
8 Founding Member offer — "lifetime" vs access Decides the launch copy above
11 Caddy AI scope Decides the Phase 2 paywall
16 Coach terms Decides whether a coaching bundle is ever viable

Kill criteria

  • If partner-funded perks require discounts that breach MAP, this is not a membership — it is a loyalty program, and should be scoped and named as one.
  • If a digital tier attracts Apple's commission, a paid tier is a web-first product or not a product at all.
  • If the Feed does not sustain weekly posting, the badge has no audience and the program should stay a quiet account flag.

The honest counter-argument

Membership is a retention answer, and Clubhouse's bottleneck today is supply: no fulfillment partner is signed, so the checkout promise is empty. The strongest case against spending any time here is opportunity cost — every hour before 15 October is better spent signing a brand. This brief is written to close the question cheaply, not to open a workstream.
