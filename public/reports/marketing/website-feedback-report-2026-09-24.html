The homepage is on brand. The preview features make claims that aren't true.

The homepage tone is right: modern, confident and golf-literate, with a clear problem ("Golf is scattered everywhere") and a working waitlist form. Its copy issues are fixable in an afternoon: one line contradicts the checkout model, and the page describes the wrong feature list.

Shop & product pages: a demo presented as a real store

"SHOP" is in the main nav and footer and opens without a login. noindex keeps it out of search, but not away from anyone who clicks through from Instagram, TikTok or LinkedIn.

Real brands that haven't signed

The shop lists Nike, Puma, adidas and FootJoy with their real model names (Air Zoom Victory Tour 3, IGNITE Articulate, Codechaos 22, Tour360 24, Pro/SL, Premiere Series). The product "image" is each brand's name set as a large wordmark. Product copy speaks for the brand: "FootJoy's best-selling spikeless shoe". Some fictional sellers reuse real product-line names too: "Tour Velvet Grips" is a Golf Pride line. The page intro says "Equipment, apparel and accessories from every partner brand in the clubhouse."

No brand has signed as a partner. Puma is a target, not a confirmed partner. Your rules say never name a partner as live until confirmed, and use partner names and imagery only with rights. As it stands, the page implies relationships that don't exist.

Made-up ratings on every product

All 26 products show a star rating and review count, about 3,900 "reviews" in total. Examples: Air Zoom Victory Tour 3 "4.7 (412 reviews)", Pro/SL "4.7 (356 reviews)", Tour Forged Irons "4.8 (96 reviews)". Clubhouse has no customers yet. Your brand rules say never invent reviews or ratings. The FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews may also apply to ratings shown to consumers. Legal to confirm

Launch-day blockers

1 · Crawlers are blocked two ways

robots.txt says Disallow: / on both chgolfco.com and www.chgolfco.com, and every page carries <meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">. That's right while the shop is a demo (see Shop). On launch day, replace robots.txt with Allow: / plus a Sitemap: line, and switch the meta tag to index, follow. Keep /cart, /checkout and /login on noindex.

2 · Every page says it's a copy of the homepage

All 33 routes (home, /shop, /cart, /checkout, /login, /privacy, /terms and /product/p1 to p26) declare <link rel="canonical" href="https://chgolfco.com">. A canonical tells Google which URL is the original. Unfixed, Google will fold the shop and every product page into the homepage and list none of them, even after noindex is removed. Developer fix: give each route its own self-referencing canonical, e.g. alternates.canonical per page in Next.js, instead of one site-wide value.

3 · The site's two addresses disagree

chgolfco.com redirects (308) to www.chgolfco.com, but the canonical, og:url, the sitemap and the JSON-LD schema all use the non-www address, so every signal points at a redirect. http://chgolfco.com takes two hops to reach the page. llms.txt uses www. Pick one host, then make metadataBase, the sitemap, the schema and llms.txt all match it. Owner decision: www or non-www

What already works

One H1 per page and a logical H2 structure. A clear title ("Clubhouse Golf | Everything Golf. One Clubhouse.", 48 characters) and meta description. Complete Open Graph and Twitter tags with a 1200×630 share image. WebSite and Organization schema linking LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok. HTTPS, a real 404 for unknown URLs, a mobile viewport and an llms.txt. That's more groundwork than most sites have before launch.
