Part 0

Verdict in one screen

The brief is right about the thing that matters and wrong about what to do first. Lead with the conclusion, as it says. Then change the order.

Holds. The next 90 days will not produce meaningful revenue, and saying so first is the right play. Affiliate cash lags the sale — worse than the brief claims. Paid tiers, display ads and selling data are all unsellable at this traffic. A single direct-sale pilot beats six. Caddy AI should stay free and sell.

Breaks. Checkout is not affiliate-only — a Stripe direct-sale rail is already built. The named pilot partner is wrong: the code has Garb Golf, not Pines and Aces. Apple and Google charge nothing on physical goods. And every page that could earn a dollar — shop, product, guides, brands — is hardcoded noindex, which outranks instrumentation as the first fix.
Missing entirely. The 90-day window is 1 Nov → 30 Jan: Black Friday, all of gift season, then the deadest month in Midwest golf retail. The brief's sequence puts the revenue work after the only peak it will see. That single omission changes the plan more than any other finding here.

Part 2

The math, rebuilt

Same method as the brief, with the verified inputs. Every line is an assumption and labelled as one — the point is the order of magnitude, and the number nobody has yet said out loud.

Per-sale earnings

Commission (verified band)    5% – 7%
Realised AOV (assumed)        $90 – $200
Per sale                        $4.50 – $14.00   (mid-point ~$9)

The brief's $10–15 is the top of that range, not the middle. At the mid-point, $1,000/month needs ~110 sales, not 70–100 — and the honest spread is 70–220.

The traffic that implies — the number to put on the screen

Target                           $1,000 / month
÷ per sale (mid)               ~110 sales
÷ click-out → sale, 1–3% (assumed)   3,700 – 11,000 click-outs
÷ session → click-out, 10–20% (assumed)   18,000 – 110,000 sessions / month
This is the slide that ends the debate. Both conversion rates are assumptions — nobody can supply the real ones, because no analytics are connected. But every plausible value lands the answer in the tens of thousands of sessions a month. A 17-day-old noindexed site does not have that, cannot have it by January, and no amount of merchant-joining changes it. Put the range up, say both rates are guesses, and ask the founders which number they would rather fund: traffic, or margin per order.

The only channel that converts a November sale into November cash is the direct-sale rail that is already half-built. That is the argument for finishing Garb, and it is stronger than the margin argument the brief makes.

Part 3

Where the plan breaks

Five findings that change what to do, ordered by how much they change it.

  1. The window is a holiday window, and the plan ignores it

    90 days from 1 November is 30 January. The brief schedules buying guides for weeks 2–6 and the direct-sale pilot for weeks 4–12 — which puts both after the only peak they will see, and new pages cannot rank inside 90 days regardless of when they ship. Golf Galaxy already sent a 2026 holiday gift guide (27 Sep notes). That asset should be pointed at this peak, this month.

    Weeks 1–4 · Nov

    Black Friday / Cyber Monday. The highest-converting fortnight in golf retail. Everything that can earn must be live by 20 Nov.

    Weeks 5–8 · Dec

    Gift season to ~20 Dec. Second and last window. Gifters buy accessories — low AOV, low commission, high volume.

    Weeks 9–13 · Jan

    Deadest month in Midwest golf. Use it to measure, reconcile the first payouts and sell sponsorships on peak numbers — not to launch.

  2. Every page that could earn is hardcoded noindex — this outranks instrumentation

    NOINDEX_ROBOTS is applied to /shop, /product/[id], /guides, /guides/[slug], /brands, /brands/[slug] and /compare. It is a literal {index:false, follow:false} — not gated on the SITE_INDEXABLE switch, and lib/site.ts documents the behaviour as deliberate. So the brief's “build buying-guide pages” is not the blocker: /guides and /guides/[slug] already exist, with a published-guides loader behind them. The blocker is that nothing there is crawlable. Confirm live before the meeting — the local repo lags production and this may already be fixed.

  3. The direct-sale pilot is already chosen, and it is not Pines and Aces

    suppliers/shopify-stores.ts contains exactly one store: Garb Golf (garb.myshopify.com), with collegiate-range exclusions confirmed by Tyler Silva on 13 Oct 2026. Pines and Aces was a reseller / Shopify-API conversation from 24 September and is not in the code. Recommending “one pilot — Pines and Aces” would open a second pilot, contradicting the brief's own “prove one flow before onboarding others.” The ask is to finish Garb: one real paid order, one real refund, one reconciled payout.

  4. The direct-sale risks are mis-weighted — tax is small, returns are not

    Missouri economic nexus is $100,000 of gross receipts over the preceding 12 months with no transaction-count trigger — unreachable in this window. Home-state Missouri collection applies from the first Missouri sale, and Stripe Tax makes that a configuration task, not a project. The costs that will actually bite a Garb pilot are returns and return shipping, chargebacks, and a support SLA: who eats a returned $220 pullover. Put it in the partner agreement, not on the engineering backlog.

  5. Caddy AI already recommends products, and the disclosure is not where the law wants it

    caddie/shop-search.ts has Caddy picking up to four products per search today. The only affiliate disclosure in the codebase is one sentence on /product/[id]. The FTC Endorsement Guides — extended to AI-generated endorsers in the 2023 revision and reiterated in 2026 — require the material connection to be disclosed at the point of recommendation, before the click. So the disclosure line ships with the tracked links, in the Caddy answer surface, not after. This is the one item in the brief that is a compliance gate rather than a growth idea.

Part 5

In the room

Push back on — all three, agreed

Paid tiers

Nothing to convert. And correct the store-fee claim while doing it: Apple and Google take nothing on gear, so “app commerce is expensive” is not the reason to say no. “No users yet” is.

Display ads

At the session volumes in Part 2, display revenue rounds to zero and costs the page speed that affiliate conversion depends on.

Selling data

No volume, and it contradicts the privacy posture the Caddy memory work was built around. Not a 90-day topic; arguably not a topic.

— and one to add: buying more affiliate networks

Six platforms are already in play. Each adds integration surface and its own minimum-payout gate. Depth on the approved advertisers beats a seventh logo.

Decisions to ask the founders for

1 · Monthly burn and the cash-runway date

As the brief says — without it nobody can judge whether any of this is enough. Ask for the date the account hits zero, not just the monthly figure.

2 · A 90-day target expressed in orders, not dollars

Dollars hide the payout lag. “50 tracked orders by 30 January” is a target the team can steer; “$1,000 of revenue” is a number the networks' payment calendars decide.

3 · Go / no-go on opening the shop to search engines

A product decision, not an engineering one — it exposes catalogue and pricing to crawlers and competitors. It is also the gate on every organic plan in the brief. Needs a named owner and a date.

4 · Who owns returns, chargebacks and the disclosure wording

Direct sales and an AI recommender both create liabilities nobody has been assigned. Name a person before the pilot takes a real card.

One-page version for the meeting: Part 0 plus the two tables in Part 2. That is the whole argument — the conclusion, the traffic number, and the payout calendar. Everything else is backup for the questions it will provoke.
