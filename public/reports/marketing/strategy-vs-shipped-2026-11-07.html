Strategy vs. Shipped

Three weeks after launch, Strategy V3 was tested against the live site. One finding governs the rest: the strategy named owned checkout as a launch requirement, warned what would happen without it, and that warning is now the shipped product. This report combines the gap analysis, a psychology layer per golfer persona, and the instrumentation tickets that would let any of it be measured — each one red-teamed in Part 4.

Strategy vs. shipped

The CTA breach is the most expensive line of copy on the site

Your own style guide forbids exactly what shipped, and the psychology explains the cost. “Add to Bag → Go to checkout” promises an on-site purchase. The golfer invests effort, reaches peak intent, and is then told to start again somewhere else and re-pick their configuration — with a disclaimer that the price may differ. That is Activation Energy spiking at the exact moment it should fall, Regret Aversion firing on the price, Commitment & Consistency broken, and the Peak-End Rule violated on the last step. The style guide anticipated all of it in one line.

Before: Add to Bag → Go to checkout
After: Buy at Golf Galaxy → — “We’ll hand you straight to Golf Galaxy to finish — they ship it and handle returns. Price confirmed at checkout.”

The competitive effect: the parity claim inverted

Strategy V3 argued owned checkout “puts Clubhouse at parity with fourteen of fifteen profiled competitors on the dimension that matters most — it keeps the customer file, the repeat-purchase data and the returns relationship rather than handing all three to a partner who is also a competitor.” It did not ship. So all three are being handed to Golf Galaxy — which the same report lists among the competitors holding a unified checkout. The precise harm it warned about is live.

Two further knock-ons: MyGolfSpy, named the primary threat, competes directly with your Buying Guides — and yours are invisible to search. And Golf Avenue’s price-anchoring idea (finding 6, “nearly free to copy”) was copied, but anchored above MSRP, which converts worse than no anchor at all.

Why the Equipment Enthusiast goes first. Your biggest psychological liability is the retailer handoff; they are the one persona who does not experience it as a loss, because they already buy across 2nd Swing, GlobalGolf, eBay and GolfWRX BST. Your one shipped differentiator — cross-retailer new-and-used comparison over ~350 brands — was built for them. They are free to reach, highest LTV, and the personas report already calls them ideal Feed seed users. Every low-knowledge persona needs powers you don’t have.

Part 3

Instrumentation tickets

Nothing in the funnel is measured today. A product view fires only page_view; Add to Bag fires nothing at all. Context: Expo / React Native Web, GA4 G-X9SXYER9WP via GTM, consent currently reporting gcs=G101. Product slugs encode the network — cj-… and awin-….

  1. Split the referral CTA P0

    Replace “Add to Bag / Go to checkout” with “Buy at {retailer} →” on referral items. Acceptance: no referral PDP renders a cart-semantics control. Implements the style guide and removes the handoff trap.

  2. Instrument the outbound click — the revenue event P0

    Custom event affiliate_click with item_id, retailer, affiliate_network (parsed from the slug prefix), price, item_category, placement (pdp / card / guide / caddy). Must fire before navigation — use sendBeacon or transport_type: 'beacon' so it survives unload. Acceptance: exactly one event per outbound navigation, visible in GA4 realtime.

  3. Pass a SubID so the networks can report back P0

    Append a SubID to every outbound affiliate URL encoding placement, content id and session — CJ uses sid, Awin uses clickref. Without this, CJ and Awin will report commissions you can never attribute to a guide, a persona or a channel. This is the highest-value marketing ticket in the list: it closes a loop GA4 cannot see, because the purchase happens off your property.

  4. Verify consent actually grants analytics Gates everything

    A consent banner is present and collection reported gcs=G101. If analytics_storage is denied or defaults unset, every event above silently does not fire. Acceptance: events confirmed for a consenting session, and documented behaviour for a non-consenting one.

  5. view_item and select_item P1

    Diagnostics, not revenue. Note for implementation: React Native Web emits no DOM-based ecommerce automatically — gtag() must be called by hand on PDP mount and on card press.

  6. add_to_cart on Add to Bag P1

    Verified firing nothing today while the badge increments. Same parameters as view_item.

  7. GA4 hygiene P2

    Mark affiliate_click as a key event, build a content → outbound funnel exploration, and add an internal-traffic filter — the 7 Nov audit sessions are already in your data.

Part 4

Red team of all three parts

Each part above, attacked. Where a claim did not survive, it is corrected in place rather than deleted.

Challenges to Parts 1–3
PartChallengeVerdictResolution
1 “Every item is a referral” was generalised from one product Narrowed Re-tested: 2 products across 2 networks (CJ and Awin) and 2 retailers, both referral; every RETAILER facet value is a third party and there is no “Clubhouse” seller option. Strong, but 3,390 items were not exhaustively checked. Stated as sampled, not universal.
1 “Feed launched empty” was rated High off one screen Corrected Scrolled: 6 posts, 6 communities, four from launch week, one with zero replies. “Seeded then stalled” is accurate; “empty” was not.
1 The competitive section is inherited, not verified Holds Every competitor fact is second-hand from a 21 Sept report. MyGolfSpy, Golf Avenue and Golfstix may all have moved in seven weeks. None was re-checked live.
2 Psychology layered on unvalidated personas Holds The personas report says it plainly: proxy-sourced, no first-party golfer data, provisional until validated against the first Founding Members. Part 2 is therefore a hypothesis stack, not findings.
2 “Start with the Equipment Enthusiast” has no supporting data Reasoned only It follows from two verified facts — the handoff exists, and that persona tolerates handoffs — but no evidence exists that any Enthusiast has ever visited. It also diverges from Strategy V3, which targets the New / Returning Golfer. If owned checkout ships, the recommendation reverts.
2 The handoff psychology is untested Holds No user research, no session recordings, no A/B test. The mechanism is well-evidenced in general; its magnitude here is assumed.
3 A spec for a team that does not exist Holds Implementation capacity is nil. These are tickets for whenever capacity appears, not a plan for this month. Nothing in Part 3 is actionable today.
3 Perfect instrumentation still will not show revenue Holds The purchase happens on the retailer’s property. GA4 can only ever show the click. Only ticket 3 (SubID) connects a commission back to a cause — which is why it outranks the GA4 events.
3 Measurement cannot explain missing commissions on its own Holds Affiliate cookie windows and cross-device buying lose conversions structurally: a golfer who clicks on mobile and buys on a laptop is uncredited no matter how well you instrument. Expect attributed revenue to understate real influence.
All Wrong constraint Holds — read this one Zero outbound clicks have ever been recorded. Theory of Constraints says fix the bottleneck, and the bottleneck is arrival, not conversion. Parts 2 and 3 optimise a funnel with no traffic. They earn their place only because the fixes must exist before traffic arrives — but the 80% is that the guides cannot be found and the nav is not crawlable.
The one free diagnostic that should precede all of this. Compare GA4 sessions since 15 Oct against the networks’ zero clicks. Sessions near zero means an arrival problem and Parts 2–3 are premature. Sessions above zero with no clicks means golfers arrived and not one chose to continue — which makes the handoff fix in Part 1 the single most urgent item in this report. It needs GA4 access, so it was not run here.
