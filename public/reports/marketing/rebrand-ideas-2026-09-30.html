Four ways Clubhouse could look

Four alternatives to the current green, ivory and gold identity on chgolfco.com. Each one keeps the brand line, Everything Golf. One Clubhouse., and is shown on the same homepage and the same comparison card, so you can compare the look and not the copy.

What the current brand gets right and wrong

What works

It's premium and unmistakably golf: deep clubhouse green, Fraunces italic and golf-native motifs (the tee-to-hole loader, the flag, the road ahead drawn as holes 1–3). Those motifs are the most ownable things you have.

The tension

The brand voice says modern, approachable, "not a country-club voice". Green, gold, serif and a crest together read as a private members' club, which is exactly the feeling the voice rejects. The gold also fails contrast at small sizes (3.23:1).

The naming problem

"Clubhouse Golf" is also used by clubhousegolf.co.uk, clubhousegolfcompany.com and clubhousegolf.nyc. A generic green-and-gold look makes that confusion worse, so the identity has to carry the recognition the name can't.

Direction A · Evolve

Clubhouse Modern

Keep the equity, lose the country club. Same green and Fraunces, but the muted gold becomes a brighter flagstick yellow, the crest becomes a simple C holding a flag, and there's much more contrast. It's the only direction that's realistic before 15 Oct.

Direction B · Reposition

Yardage Book

Clubhouse as the golfer's yardage book: the well-worn notebook every serious player trusts to make decisions. Grid paper, condensed caps, monospaced numbers and a flag-red marker. It visually says compare and decide, which is the one thing nobody else offers.

Recommendation

  1. Don't rebrand 15 days before launch. Ship A · Clubhouse Modern now. It's a token-level update that also fixes the contrast failures in the UI/UX audit, and it keeps everyone's recognition of the current site intact.
  2. Test B · Yardage Book after launch as the long-term identity. It's the only direction whose look carries your wedge (cross-brand comparison with a checkout attached). Pilot it on the comparison view and the buying guides first.
  3. Use C · Sunday Sessions for the Clubhouse Feed and organic social, where energy matters more than trust signals.
  4. Before committing to anything, run a 5-second test with 5–8 golfers from each persona: show each homepage for 5 seconds, then ask "What is this, and who is it for?"

What these are: concept directions, not finished logos. The marks are quick sketches to show a signature idea; a final mark needs a designer and a trademark check. Every palette pair shown was checked against WCAG 2.x AA. Prices and options in the mockups are placeholders.
